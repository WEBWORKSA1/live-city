Relocation

How to compare cities before you move: a data-first method

"Which city should I move to?" is really ten smaller questions with different weights for different people. This method turns those questions into a single score you can defend — and it takes about an evening.

Step 1: Pick 6–8 criteria that change your daily life

Avoid the temptation to track everything. Choose the factors that will affect an ordinary Tuesday, not a holiday. A good starting set:

  • Housing cost relative to your expected income (rent or mortgage as a share of take-home pay).
  • Income potential in your field, including remote-work options.
  • Climate fit — number of months you'd enjoy being outside.
  • Air quality across the year, not just on the day you check.
  • Commute — realistic door-to-door time from affordable neighbourhoods.
  • Safety and general sense of ease.
  • Time zone relative to family, clients or team.
  • Lifestyle — food, culture, nature, community in your language.

Step 2: Weight them before you look at any city

Give each criterion a weight so the total adds up to 100. Do this before scoring cities, otherwise you'll unconsciously tune weights to favour the city you already like. A remote software developer might weight housing cost 25, climate 15, air quality 10, time zone 15, lifestyle 15, safety 10, commute 5 and income 5. A family moving for a hospital job might put commute and schools far higher.

Step 3: Score each city from 1 to 10

Use the same sources for every city so comparisons are fair. For climate and air quality, look at both today's live readings and the best-time-to-visit notes on each city dashboard; a single sunny day tells you little. For time zone, our comparison tool shows the exact difference and the overlap with your current city. For housing, compare like with like: the same size of home in neighbourhoods with similar commute times.

Step 4: Multiply and add

For each city, multiply each score by its weight and add the results. The maximum is 1,000. Here's a worked example for a remote worker comparing two cities:

City B is cheaper, but City A wins because the time-zone fit matters a lot to this person. That's the point of the method: it makes trade-offs visible.

Step 5: Stress-test the winner

Before committing, run three checks. First, change your top weight by ten points — if the winner flips, the decision is closer than it looks. Second, check the worst month: look at the hottest, wettest or most polluted time of year. Third, if you can, spend a week there living normally rather than as a tourist.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing a city centre in one place with a suburb in another.
  • Ignoring one-off moving costs, which can equal several months of savings.
  • Over-weighting a great holiday memory.
  • Forgetting how far you'll be from the people you'll want to visit.
