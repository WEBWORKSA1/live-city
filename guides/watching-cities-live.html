Live cams

Watching cities live: a guide to public webcams

Live city cams turn a screen into a window. They're relaxing to watch, surprisingly useful for planning and a great way to get a feel for a place before you go — or before you move.

Where city streams come from

Most public city cams are run by four kinds of operators: tourism boards and city governments showing off landmarks; hotels, restaurants and building owners who host a camera with a great view; dedicated webcam networks that install and maintain cameras around the world; and individual enthusiasts streaming from a balcony or window. Many of them broadcast on YouTube, which is why Live.City links each city to a filtered search of live streams.

How to tell if a stream is really live

  • Look for YouTube's red "LIVE" badge and a current viewer count.
  • Compare the light with the city's local time and sunrise or sunset on its dashboard. If it's daylight in the stream but 2 am locally, it's a replay.
  • Check the weather: rain or clouds in the stream should match the live conditions.
  • Watch for on-screen clocks, traffic and people moving naturally.

Useful ways to use live cams

Before a trip. Check crowds at a landmark at different times of day, see how people are dressed and judge whether a view is worth the queue.

Before a move. Watch a neighbourhood's main street on a weekday evening and a weekend morning. You'll learn more about its rhythm than from any brochure.

For events. New Year's Eve countdowns, festivals, marathons and parades are often streamed from multiple angles.

For weather. A beach or mountain cam is the fastest way to see whether fog, snow or sun is actually arriving.

For calm. Many people simply keep a quiet harbour or street view running while they work.

Etiquette and privacy

Reputable public cams are placed to show landmarks and wide views, not to identify individuals. Don't record or share footage to single out people, and respect any rules the stream owner sets in chat. If you run a camera yourself, point it at public spaces in a way that avoids private windows and gardens, and follow local laws.

Why some streams disappear

Cameras go offline for maintenance, bad weather, power cuts or the end of a sponsorship. Popular streams also sometimes restart with a new video link, which breaks old embeds. That's why our featured stream on the Live Now page loads a channel's current broadcast, and why city pages search for fresh streams each time.
